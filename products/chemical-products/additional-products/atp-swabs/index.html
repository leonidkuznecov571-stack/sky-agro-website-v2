ATP Swabs

Rapid cleanliness verification

ATP Swabs are rapid hygiene monitoring devices for verifying the cleanliness of brewery surfaces and CIP rinse water. They detect residual organic matter in seconds, providing objective evidence that cleaning has been effective before equipment returns to service.

ATP Swabs are rapid hygiene verification devices used alongside a luminometer to detect residual organic matter on brewery surfaces and in CIP rinse water. They provide immediate, objective evidence of cleaning effectiveness, allowing brewers to verify that vessels, pipework, fittings, and contact surfaces are clean before being returned to service.

These swabs work by detecting adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the energy molecule present in all organic material and living cells. After swabbing the target surface or sampling rinse water, the swab is activated to release a bioluminescent reagent that reacts with any ATP present, producing light measured by a luminometer. The result is reported in Relative Light Units (RLU) within seconds. Higher readings indicate higher levels of organic residue, which may include yeast, beer residue, soil deposits, or biofilm; lower readings indicate effective cleaning. This converts a previously visual, subjective assessment of cleanliness into a quantifiable, repeatable measurement.

For breweries, ATP testing is a powerful tool for validating CIP cycles, identifying weak points in cleaning programmes (vessel manways, valve clusters, sample ports, and other hard-to-reach areas), and demonstrating due diligence during audits. They are particularly useful for spot-checking after a CIP run, troubleshooting when product quality issues arise, and building a benchmark dataset of expected RLU readings across the brewery. While ATP testing does not replace microbiological culture or specific allergen testing, it provides a fast first line of defence and significantly reduces the risk of returning a poorly cleaned vessel to production.

ATP Swabs are suitable for:

  • CIP cycle validation across vessels, pipework, and heat exchangers
  • Surface hygiene verification on contact equipment
  • Rinse water testing to confirm cleaning effectiveness
  • Identifying weak points in cleaning programmes
  • HACCP and quality assurance documentation
  • Audit preparation and demonstrating due diligence
  • Troubleshooting suspected contamination issues
  • Routine sanitation programme monitoring
